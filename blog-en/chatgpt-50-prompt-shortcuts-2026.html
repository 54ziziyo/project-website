You've probably seen posts claiming ChatGPT has "hidden commands" — tags like "/human" or "/think" you can drop at the start of a prompt to instantly get better answers. Let's clear something up first: these aren't secret features OpenAI built into the system. They're user-invented shorthand that ChatGPT interprets as instructions — putting a short tag in front of your question nudges the model's angle, depth, or tone.

Not official, but the underlying techniques are genuinely effective, and many are backed by OpenAI's own documentation or established research (chain-of-thought, role-play, devil's advocate, and more). Here are 50 tags organized by use case, with sources so you can trust what's actually working here.

1. Deep Thinking

  1. /think — Have the AI reason through the problem before answering, instead of giving a surface-level response
    ChatGPT Prompt Example
    /think My e-commerce site is running a limited Halloween flash sale next week, expecting a 50x traffic spike, and the current setup is a single database —how should I optimize it to avoid crashing?
  2. /alt3 — Get three distinct angles on the same answer, so you can compare (adjustable to /alt4, /alt5 — cap it at /alt5, since beyond that the angles start overlapping and get harder to digest)
    ChatGPT Prompt Example
    /alt3 I'm sellinga caffeine-free herbal black bean tea marketed for better sleep. Write one attention-grabbing Instagram ad line for it.
  3. /blind-spot — Have it proactively surface what you might be missing
    ChatGPT Prompt Example
    /blind-spot I'm planning to launchan Instagram account for Japan-sourced motorcycle gear reselling. What risks might I be overlooking in this plan?
  4. /devil — Play devil's advocate, specifically hunting for holes in your argument
    ChatGPT Prompt Example
    /devil I thinkraising prices by 20% won't hurt sales. Argue against this.
  5. /root-cause — Dig down to the actual cause, not just the surface symptom
    ChatGPT Prompt Example
    /root-cause Recentlymy Instagram post reach suddenly dropped by half. What could be the root cause?
  6. /first-principles — Rebuild the answer from first principles instead of reaching for a stock response
    ChatGPT Prompt Example
    /first-principles Don't copy what competitors do — designa cost-minimal payment and logistics solution for a reselling business from first principles.
  7. /worst-case — Lay out the worst-case scenario along with a contingency plan
    ChatGPT Prompt Example
    /worst-case I'm about to signa one-year exclusive distribution deal with a supplier. Lay out the worst-case scenarios and contingency plans.
  8. /assumptions — List the hidden assumptions baked into this answer
    ChatGPT Prompt Example
    /assumptions I assumemy followers are mostly women aged 20-30. What unverified assumptions might be baked into that belief?

Do these tags actually have scientific backing?

To be honest: among these 50, some are mature techniques with solid research or official documentation behind them, while others are just well-known communication conventions repackaged as tags — equally useful in practice, but without a dedicated study validating the "tag name" itself.

It's worth separating "tag name" from "underlying technique" — these sit at two different levels of verifiability. The techniques themselves (chain-of-thought, devil's advocate, role-play, ELI5) genuinely have research or official documentation behind them. But the tag name itself is just whatever a given creator happened to call it — there's no standard, and there's no way to "verify" that a particular name is the correct one. Different roundups online use different tag names for the exact same technique, and you're free to rename any of these yourself, as long as the model understands what you mean.

  • Chain-of-thought (behind /think, /step-by-step): A widely validated technique — having the model reason through steps before answering leads to more accurate, better-justified responses.
  • Devil's advocate (behind /devil): Demonstrated in practice to turn ChatGPT from a compliant assistant into a critical partner that surfaces logical gaps.
  • Role-play (behind /persona, /boss-mode): OpenAI's own best-practice guidance recommends giving the model an explicit role (e.g. "You are a senior financial analyst") to get answers closer to the expertise angle you actually want.
  • ELI5, brevity requests (behind /eli5, /tldr, /no-fluff): These are long-standing communication conventions (ELI5 originates from Reddit's r/explainlikeimfive) — there's no dedicated AI research paper validating the name itself, but "ask for simpler language" is a request anyone, including the model, understands and executes effectively.

This "package prompting techniques as slash tags" approach isn't unique to this post either — other similar roundups exist online (such as a 32-shortcut ChatGPT cheat-code article), which you can cross-reference.

Bottom line

These tags aren't officially certified "system commands" — they're custom shorthand that the AI happens to understand. Drop the tag at the front of your prompt and it nudges the response style in that direction. The real quality of the output still depends on how clearly you describe the actual question — the tags just save you from retyping the same instruction pattern every time.
