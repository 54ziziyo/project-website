You've probably seen posts claiming ChatGPT has "hidden commands" — tags like "/human" or "/think" you can drop at the start of a prompt to instantly get better answers. Let's clear something up first: these aren't secret features OpenAI built into the system. They're user-invented shorthand that ChatGPT interprets as instructions — putting a short tag in front of your question nudges the model's angle, depth, or tone.

Not official, but the underlying techniques are genuinely effective, and many are backed by OpenAI's own documentation or established research (chain-of-thought, role-play, devil's advocate, and more). Here are 50 tags organized by use case, with sources so you can trust what's actually working here.

1. Deep Thinking

  1. /think — Have the AI reason through the problem before answering, instead of giving a surface-level response
    Example: /think My e-commerce site is running a limited Halloween flash sale next week, and I'm expecting a 50x traffic spike. The current setup is a single database — how should I optimize it to avoid crashing?
  2. /alt3 — Get three distinct angles on the same answer, so you can compare (adjustable to /alt4, /alt5 — cap it at /alt5, since beyond that the angles start overlapping and get harder to digest)
    Example: /alt3 I'm selling a caffeine-free herbal black bean tea marketed for better sleep. Write one attention-grabbing Instagram ad line for it.
  3. /blind-spot — Have it proactively surface what you might be missing
    Example: /blind-spot I'm planning to launch an Instagram account for Japan-sourced motorcycle gear reselling. What risks might I be overlooking in this plan?
  4. /devil — Play devil's advocate, specifically hunting for holes in your argument
    Example: /devil I think raising prices by 20% won't hurt sales. Argue against this.
  5. /root-cause — Dig down to the actual cause, not just the surface symptom
    Example: /root-cause My Instagram post reach suddenly dropped by half recently. What could be the root cause?
  6. /first-principles — Rebuild the answer from first principles instead of reaching for a stock response
    Example: /first-principles Don't copy what competitors do — design a cost-minimal payment and logistics solution for a reselling business from first principles.
  7. /worst-case — Lay out the worst-case scenario along with a contingency plan
    Example: /worst-case I'm about to sign a one-year exclusive distribution deal with a supplier. Lay out the worst-case scenarios and contingency plans.
  8. /assumptions — List the hidden assumptions baked into this answer
    Example: /assumptions I assume my followers are mostly women aged 20-30. What unverified assumptions might be baked into that belief?

2. Communication & Writing

  1. /no-fluff — Cut all the filler, keep only what matters
    Example: /no-fluff For a small e-commerce site choosing a payment gateway, compare only fees, approval speed, and integration difficulty in a Markdown table.
  2. /human — Answer in a more natural, less robotic voice
    Example: /human Share, in first person, a funny little story about "my first solo trip to Japan, where I fell asleep on the train and rode it all the way to the last stop."
  3. /eli5 — Explain it like I'm 5
    Example: /eli5 What is an API key, and why is it dangerous if it leaks?
  4. /tone-casual — Switch to a relaxed, conversational tone
    Example: /tone-casual Rewrite this product announcement in a casual, friend-to-friend tone.
  5. /tone-formal — Switch to a formal, professional tone
    Example: /tone-formal Rewrite this payment reminder message in a formal but polite tone.
  6. /shorten — Cut the length in half while keeping the key points
    Example: /shorten Cut this product description's length in half without losing the key selling points.
  7. /bullet — Present everything as bullet points, no long paragraphs
    Example: /bullet Reformat the steps in this tutorial article as bullet points instead of long paragraphs.
  8. /story — Explain through a story or analogy instead of listing facts
    Example: /story Explain, through a story rather than a list of rules, why resellers use tiered exchange-rate markups.

3. Decision-Making & Analysis

  1. /pros-cons — Produce a pros-and-cons comparison table
    Example: /pros-cons List a pros-and-cons comparison table for Supplier A's and Supplier B's terms.
  2. /decision-matrix — Use a scoring matrix to help you decide
    Example: /decision-matrix I'm deciding whether to run Instagram ads. Use a scoring matrix to help me evaluate it.
  3. /risk — Surface the potential risks in this decision
    Example: /risk I'm planning to switch all products to a pre-order model. Surface the potential risks in this decision.
  4. /compare — Compare the differences between two options
    Example: /compare Compare the differences between building a store on WooCommerce versus Shopify.
  5. /prioritize — Rank multiple tasks by priority
    Example: /prioritize Prioritize these five to-dos: listing new products, replying to a complaint, reviewing ad reports, restocking, and product photography.

4. Creativity & Brainstorming

  1. /brainstorm — Generate a large volume of ideas without filtering
    Example: /brainstorm Brainstorm 20 content ideas for Instagram Stories, without filtering any out yet.
  2. /wild — Give me one wild, impractical, but genuinely inspiring idea
    Example: /wild Give me one wild, impractical but genuinely inspiring marketing idea for a reselling business.
  3. /remix — Mash up two unrelated concepts to generate something new
    Example: /remix Mash up "Japanese local festivals" and "motorcycle gear unboxing" — two unrelated concepts — into one content idea.
  4. /alternative — Give three completely different alternative directions
    Example: /alternative For naming this new product, give me three completely different alternative directions.

5. Learning & Understanding

  1. /teach-me — Use the Socratic method to guide me to the answer myself, instead of just telling me
    Example: /teach-me I want to understand how customs duty is calculated. Guide me to work it out myself using the Socratic method, don't just give me the answer.
  2. /quiz-me — Quiz me on what I just learned
    Example: /quiz-me Quiz me on the Yoast SEO rules I just learned.
  3. /analogy — Explain the abstract concept through a relatable analogy
    Example: /analogy Explain "what is an API" using an everyday analogy.
  4. /step-by-step — Break it down into a concrete, actionable sequence of steps
    Example: /step-by-step Break down "how to apply for Meta Business Verification" into concrete steps.

6. Efficiency & Formatting

  1. /table — Present the content as a table
    Example: /table Organize the specs of these camera models into a table.
  2. /tldr — Give me a summary in three sentences or fewer
    Example: /tldr Give me a three-sentence summary of this long contract clause.
  3. /checklist — Convert this into a checkable checklist
    Example: /checklist Convert this "new product listing SOP" into a checkable checklist.
  4. /template — Give me a ready-to-use template
    Example: /template Give me a reusable template for Instagram post captions.

7. Business & Work

  1. /email — Rewrite this as a professional, polished email
    Example: /email Rewrite this complaint-toned message as a professional, polished email to a supplier.
  2. /pitch — Frame this as a 30-second elevator pitch
    Example: /pitch Introduce my reselling brand as a 30-second elevator pitch.
  3. /negotiate — Give me talking points for this negotiation scenario
    Example: /negotiate My supplier wants to raise prices. Give me talking points I can use in the negotiation.
  4. /boss-mode — Review this the way a demanding boss would
    Example: /boss-mode Review this month's marketing plan the way a demanding boss would.
  5. /meeting-notes — Turn this conversation into formatted meeting notes
    Example: /meeting-notes Turn this conversation with a supplier into formatted meeting notes.
  6. /objection — Predict likely objections and prepare responses to them
    Example: /objection Customers might object that reselling prices are higher than Japan's official site. Predict their objections and prepare responses.

8. Code & Technical

  1. /debug — Find where this code is likely to break
    Example: /debug This Nuxt API route keeps returning 500 errors. Help me find where it's likely breaking.
  2. /explain-code — Explain this code line by line
    Example: /explain-code Explain this scraper code line by line.
  3. /refactor — Improve this code's readability and efficiency
    Example: /refactor Refactor this repeatedly-duplicated product pricing calculation logic.
  4. /edge-case — List the edge cases this logic might be missing
    Example: /edge-case What edge cases might this order-total calculation logic be missing (e.g. zero-dollar orders, negative discounts)?

9. Emotional Support & Growth

  1. /reframe — Reframe this in a more constructive, positive light
    Example: /reframe The negotiation with my supplier fell through. Reframe this in a more constructive, positive light.
  2. /vent — Just let me vent first, don't jump straight to advice
    Example: /vent I'm exhausted from handling customer complaints today. Just let me vent first, don't jump to advice.
  3. /coach — Ask coaching questions to help me clarify my goal, instead of giving direct advice
    Example: /coach I'm unsure whether to pivot my reselling business into a private-label brand. Use coaching questions to help me clarify my goal.

10. Advanced Combinations

  1. /chain — Break the task into multiple steps and guide me through each one
    Example: /chain Break "planning a new-product launch livestream" into multiple steps and guide me through each one.
  2. /persona — Answer as if you were a specific role or expert
    Example: /persona Assume you're a senior e-commerce consultant, and review this operations report from that perspective.
  3. /socratic — Keep questioning me back until I work out the answer myself
    Example: /socratic On the question of "should I raise prices," keep questioning me back until I work out the answer myself.
  4. /source — Require the answer to be backed by cited credibility or sources, not asserted freely
    Example: /source You mentioned an average e-commerce conversion rate for Taiwan — cite the source behind that number.

Do these tags actually have scientific backing?

To be honest: among these 50, some are mature techniques with solid research or official documentation behind them, while others are just well-known communication conventions repackaged as tags — equally useful in practice, but without a dedicated study validating the "tag name" itself.

It's worth separating "tag name" from "underlying technique" — these sit at two different levels of verifiability. The techniques themselves (chain-of-thought, devil's advocate, role-play, ELI5) genuinely have research or official documentation behind them. But the tag name itself is just whatever a given creator happened to call it — there's no standard, and there's no way to "verify" that a particular name is the correct one. Different roundups online use different tag names for the exact same technique, and you're free to rename any of these yourself, as long as the model understands what you mean.

  • Chain-of-thought (behind /think, /step-by-step): A widely validated technique — having the model reason through steps before answering leads to more accurate, better-justified responses.
  • Devil's advocate (behind /devil): Demonstrated in practice to turn ChatGPT from a compliant assistant into a critical partner that surfaces logical gaps.
  • Role-play (behind /persona, /boss-mode): OpenAI's own best-practice guidance recommends giving the model an explicit role (e.g. "You are a senior financial analyst") to get answers closer to the expertise angle you actually want.
  • ELI5, brevity requests (behind /eli5, /tldr, /no-fluff): These are long-standing communication conventions (ELI5 originates from Reddit's r/explainlikeimfive) — there's no dedicated AI research paper validating the name itself, but "ask for simpler language" is a request anyone, including the model, understands and executes effectively.

This "package prompting techniques as slash tags" approach isn't unique to this post either — other similar roundups exist online (such as a 32-shortcut ChatGPT cheat-code article), which you can cross-reference.

Bottom line

These tags aren't officially certified "system commands" — they're custom shorthand that the AI happens to understand. Drop the tag at the front of your prompt and it nudges the response style in that direction. The real quality of the output still depends on how clearly you describe the actual question — the tags just save you from retyping the same instruction pattern every time.
