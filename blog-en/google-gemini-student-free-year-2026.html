Google is offering eligible college students a free year of Google AI Plus, and the news has been spreading fast in student circles. I went through the official details myself and found a few things that differ from what's being reshared around — most notably that the Taiwan plan isn't the same as the US one, plus a few billing details that could catch you out later. Here's the full picture.

⚠️ First, the most important thing: a lot of posts say "get Google AI Pro free (worth $19.99/month)" — that's the US student plan. Taiwanese students get Google AI Plus, normally NT$165/month. They're different plans, so don't anchor on that US dollar figure.

What Taiwanese students actually get

  • Access to the Gemini Omni model
  • 2x the usage limits of a free account
  • 400GB of Google Drive storage (honestly the most practical part — it's essentially a Google One storage tier)
  • Free for one year

For comparison: US students get AI Pro, which includes 5TB of storage, 4x usage limits, and Gemini built into Gmail and Google Docs. The Taiwan version is the lighter tier — but 400GB plus doubled limits, free for a year, is still a genuinely good deal.

What if I'm Taiwanese but studying abroad?

I checked Google's official documentation on this one, and the answer is clear: it depends on which country your school is in, not your nationality.

Verification runs through SheerID using your school email address, so what the system recognizes is your institution — not your passport.

  • Taiwanese national + enrolled at a US school → you go through the US offer and get AI Pro (normally $19.99/month, with 5TB storage, 4x usage limits, and Gemini integrated into Gmail and Google Docs) — a noticeably better tier than Taiwan's AI Plus
  • Taiwanese national + enrolled in Taiwan → the AI Plus plan described above (400GB, 2x limits)
  • Non-Taiwanese + enrolled in Taiwan → also the Taiwan plan

⚠️ A few regions are excluded entirely, which catches people out: Canada, Hong Kong, and Macau, along with Albania, Bolivia, and Tunisia — schools in these places don't qualify for any of the student offers. Plenty of Taiwanese students study in Canada and Hong Kong, so if that's you, unfortunately this one isn't available — no point going through the signup flow.

Who's eligible?

  • Students currently enrolled at a Taiwanese college or university (18+; the official page handles final verification)
  • You must apply with a personal Google account (not a school-issued one) and complete student verification
  • You can verify using your school email address
  • If you've claimed a student plan before, or currently have another Google subscription running, you'll need to wait until those end before applying

How to claim it

  1. Sign in with your personal Google account
  2. Go to the student plan page: one.google.com/ai-student
  3. Verify your student status with your school email
  4. Enter a payment method (you can't skip this — more on why below)
  5. Once confirmed, your free year is activated

⚠️ Three catches worth knowing

1. You have to add a payment method to claim it

Even though the plan is free, you must provide a valid payment method (credit card, etc.) at signup. If you're not comfortable adding a card, this offer isn't really workable for you — there's no way around this step.

2. It auto-renews at NT$165/month after the free year

This is the easiest way to get caught out — the plan doesn't stop when the free year ends, it auto-renews and starts charging NT$165/month. If you only want the free year, you need to cancel it yourself before it expires. My advice: the moment you claim it, set a calendar reminder for one year out, because you will absolutely forget otherwise.

3. The claim deadline is December 31, 2026

After that date, the offer is gone. If you're eligible but undecided, you can claim it now and sit on it (just pair that with the reminder from point 2) rather than waiting until you actually need it.

The new study features launching alongside it

Beyond the plan itself, Google shipped several student-focused features that seem genuinely useful if you're prepping for exams or writing papers. The videos below are from Google's official blog:

Student Hub

There's now a "Students" entry in the Gemini sidebar that gathers the study-related tools in one place, so you're not rethinking which tool to use every time.

Study notebooks: breaking material into bite-sized lessons

Drop your course material in and it breaks it down into small chunks, then generates flashcards and practice quizzes automatically. For midterms and finals, this is much faster than organizing everything from scratch.

Interactive 3D visualizations

Abstract concepts (biological structures, physics principles) can be rendered as interactive 3D models — far more intuitive than a text explanation.

Deep Research × Gemini Live: research by talking it through

This is the one I find most interesting: Deep Research hooked up to Gemini Live, so you can keep asking follow-up questions conversationally instead of firing off one question and waiting for an answer. It keeps your train of thought intact while researching.

There's also calendar integration to sync exam dates and assignment deadlines.

So is it worth claiming?

If you're eligible, yes — but set that cancellation reminder. The reasoning is simple: 400GB of cloud storage free for a year is meaningful on its own if your Drive is permanently full, and the doubled Gemini limits are almost a bonus on top.

The honest caveat: this "free year, then auto-renew" structure is designed so that you get used to it and stay on as a paying customer. That's not a conspiracy — it's a completely normal business model — but you should go in knowing the rules rather than being surprised by a charge later. Claim it, then set the reminder. That's all it takes.
