Quick question first: when you buy something, which moment feels better — the instant you hit "order," or the moment you actually unbox the package?

I'm asking because I recently saw several posts claiming a trend called "dopamine sites" is spreading in South Korea — websites where nothing actually happens, yet you get the full emotional experience of the buying process anyway. I tracked down two real, playable sites and tried them myself. Here's the rundown.

What is a "dopamine site"?

This phenomenon originated in South Korea, particularly amid rising living costs and heavy pressure on younger generations. The core logic: dopamine is actually released in anticipation of a reward, not at the moment you receive it — which is why the simple act of browsing, adding to cart, and checking out can deliver a rush close to real shopping, even when nothing ever ships to your door.

1. Fake Shopping: Dopamine Shop

Site: dopamine-shop.com

Dopamine Shop is a parody/entertainment virtual shopping site built around one idea: "enjoy the feel and thrill of browsing a store, without spending a cent."

Key features:

  • Zero-cost shopping fix: Every product, price, and discount on the site is fictional. Browse freely, add to cart, and simulate checkout to get the dopamine hit — with no charges and no real package ever arriving
  • A huge range of fake products: Covers tech, gaming gear, home & kitchen, beauty and fashion, and more
  • Virtual raffles/mystery boxes: Standard, premium, and legendary-tier mystery boxes with disclosed odds, free to open
  • Framed as an impulse-spending remedy: Inspired by Korea's popular "dopamine site (도파민 사이트)" trend, designed to help people relieve the urge to shop without triggering real-world impulse purchases

2. Fake Delivery: FoodNeverComes

Site: foodnevercomes.com

FoodNeverComes is a stress-relief/novelty site built to give people the rush of ordering food online — for free. The core idea: satisfy the craving to order takeout (and get the dopamine hit), without spending a dime.

How it works:

  • Fake ordering, real stress relief: Browse menus from 12 countries (Japan, Korea, Italy, Mexico, and more) just like a real delivery app, and add whatever you're craving to your cart
  • No charges whatsoever: At checkout, the site auto-fills a fake credit card — no personal info, no payment required
  • Hilarious delivery tracking: After checkout, a live map and tracking screen pops up — your courier is literally "Waddles the Penguin," and the process is full of playful, absurd messages
  • Real recipes included: Nothing actually arrives, but every dish comes with a free, simple recipe in case you actually get hungry watching it

My take

Honestly, after trying both sites, I'm not entirely sure why people would use them. For me, the moment I actually receive something and unbox it beats the moment I click "order," every time. Then again, that might just be because I personally enjoy the wait-and-anticipation part of ordering — while these sites are engineered to deliver the rush with zero wait and zero cost. Two different kinds of "high," fundamentally.

Which camp are you in — the "ordering" high, or the "unboxing" high? If you want to try these out yourself, let me know in the comments, and tell me afterward whether you actually found any real value in them.
